Earthquake Watch — Five M5+ Quakes in Under Three Hours off Vanuatu

By The Discovery Wire

Illustration by The Discovery Wire

The ground off Vanuatu did not move once this morning. It moved five times in under three hours.

A strong earthquake registered by the USGS as magnitude 6.0 struck 214 kilometers (133 miles) northwest of Port-Olry, Vanuatu, at 02:21 UTC on October 28, 2026, at a shallow depth of about 10 kilometers. EMSC reported the same magnitude and depth.

It was not alone. The sequence, straight from the public USGS-tracked reporting:

  • M5.5 at 01:39 UTC, about 221 km northwest of Port-Olry
  • M5.2 at 02:17 UTC, about 220 km northwest of Port-Olry — roughly four and a half minutes before the M6.0
  • M6.0 at 02:21 UTC, 214 km northwest of Port-Olry
  • M5.6 at 02:59 UTC, about 174 km northwest of Port-Olry — about 38 minutes after the M6.0
  • M5.0 at 04:31 UTC, about 200 km west of Sola

Including the M6.0, the USGS recorded five events of magnitude 5.0 or greater over approximately two hours and 52 minutes, all listed at a depth of approximately 10 km.

What the alert level means

The USGS issued a Green alert for shaking-related fatalities and economic losses — a low likelihood of casualties and damage. The epicenter was far offshore: 256 km northwest of Luganville and 523 km northwest of Port-Vila. That distance is the main reason a shallow M6.0 can stay quiet on land.

The same area was struck by M6.9 and M6.3 events on October 24, so this is a restless stretch of plate boundary, not a single isolated jolt.

The preparedness part

We don't forecast earthquakes here — nobody can. We just read the instruments out loud. Every cluster like this is the same unglamorous reminder: the useful thing to own is not a prediction, it's a go-bag — water, food for 72 hours, a first-aid kit, a flashlight, and copies of your documents. The kit we keep coming back to in these watches is a compact waterproof first-aid and survival kit — the kind that lives in a car trunk or by the door and is forgotten until the day it isn't.

If today's log prompts you to check yours, that's the whole point of writing it down.
