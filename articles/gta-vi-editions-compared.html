GTA VI Editions Compared: $79.99, $99.99, a $20 Upgrade — and a $399.99 Box With No Game In It

By The Discovery Wire

Illustration by The Discovery Wire

GTA VI Editions Compared: $79.99, $99.99, a $20 Upgrade — and a $399.99 Box With No Game In It

Search "GTA VI collector's edition" this week and you will find a $399.99 box that looks like the ultimate version of the game. It is not a version of the game at all. Here is the whole lineup, with US prices as announced by Take-Two on June 24, 2026 (pre-orders opened June 25), so nobody pays collector money for a box of merchandise by mistake.

The lineup at a glance

  • Standard Edition — $79.99. The full game. Digital, or physical as a code in a box (no disc — see our companion piece). This is the complete story, the full map, the whole game.
  • Ultimate Edition — $99.99. The full game plus in-game extras. Digital only — there is no physical Ultimate.
  • Ultimate Edition Upgrade — $20. Bought later, it adds the Ultimate extras to a Standard copy.
  • The Goodtime State – Vice City Collection — $399.99. A merchandise box from the Rockstar Store. The game is sold separately — the listing says so plainly. No code, no disc, no game.

What the extra $20 for Ultimate actually buys

Both editions are the same game: same story, same Leonida, same release moment. The Ultimate Edition's premium buys digital extras threaded through Jason and Lucia's story — exclusive vehicles, weapons, and outfits, five shops that only open for Ultimate owners, and two exclusive side mission lines (listed as "jobs" in some store copy). Nothing in the main campaign is held back from Standard players; the choice is purely about the extras.

The upgrade path removes the pressure

Because the Ultimate upgrade is sold separately for $20, Standard and Ultimate converge at the same $99.99 total either way. There is no launch-day penalty for starting Standard and deciding later — buy the $79.99 edition, play, and if the exclusive shops and side missions start calling, the upgrade is there at the same effective price. Physical buyers: redeem your code first, then the upgrade attaches to that copy.

One bonus applies to both editions: buy before November 20, 2026 and the Vintage Vice City Pack is included. Miss that window and the pack is the thing you actually lost by waiting — not money on the edition choice.

The $399.99 box, item by item

The product generating the "collector's edition" confusion is officially Grand Theft Auto VI: The Goodtime State – Vice City Collection, sold directly by the Rockstar Store since September 24, 2026. For $399.99 (£349.99 in the UK) you get 11 pieces of merchandise, including Oakley Frogskins sunglasses, a New Era cap, a Macca the Gator figure, a Leonida map poster, a crossbody bag, and pins. It is limited to one per person, ships from November 19, 2026, and ships to 23 countries in North America and Europe, with shipping extra.

The math that matters: game plus box runs $479.98 with Standard or $499.98 with Ultimate, before shipping and tax. Anyone budgeting $399.99 as their all-in launch spend is about to have a bad evening.

Who should buy what

  • Most players: Standard, $79.99. The complete game, and the upgrade keeps the Ultimate door open at no penalty.
  • Players who will live in Leonida for months: Ultimate, $99.99. Five exclusive shops and two extra mission lines is real content if you'll actually roam for it.
  • Collectors: the Vice City Collection, knowingly. It's a genuinely limited merchandise set — just budget the game on top of it, and order the game separately so launch night isn't spent staring at a very nice pair of sunglasses.

Reference: Take-Two / Rockstar Games edition announcement, Jun 24, 2026; official Rockstar Store listing for The Goodtime State – Vice City Collection (read in full via Timesaver's listing report, Oct 24, 2026); console store listings for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, Nov 2026. US prices; regional pricing varies.
