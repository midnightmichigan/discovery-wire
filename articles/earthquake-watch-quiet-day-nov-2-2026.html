Earthquake Watch — A Quiet Day That Still Logged 204 Quakes

By The Discovery Wire

Illustration by The Discovery Wire

The planet had a quiet day — if you can call 204 earthquakes quiet.

Here is the seismic log from the public USGS catalog for the 24 hours from 7:27 a.m. EST on November 1 to 7:27 a.m. EST on November 2, as summarized in Orbis Weather's daily report: 20 earthquakes of magnitude 4.5 or larger, none above 5.1, no USGS alerts and no tsunami flags. The catalog counted 204 earthquakes of all sizes worldwide in that window.

Four quakes tied at the top

  • M5.1 at 08:13 UTC on November 2, 19 km west-northwest of Tomohon, Indonesia — deep, at about 197 km
  • M5.1 at 01:18 UTC, Kermadec Islands region — 10 km deep
  • M5.1 at 00:53 UTC, Banda Sea — about 94 km deep
  • M5.1 at 17:11 UTC on November 1, South Sandwich Islands region — about 108 km deep

All four were far from major cities. A big number is not the same thing as a big event — depth and distance decide what anyone on the surface actually feels.

The pattern in the noise

Five of the 20 events struck the Mid-Indian Ridge, a remote spreading ridge in the middle of the Indian Ocean, far from land. Three of those five came within 23 minutes of each other on the evening of November 1. That is the Earth quietly rearranging its own floor, in a place almost nobody will ever stand.

Two quakes also struck the Luzon Strait off Basco, Philippines — a magnitude 4.6 and a 4.7, about 14 hours apart.

Before and after the window

Just before the window opened, at 11:47 UTC on November 1, a magnitude 6.0 struck 165 km northwest of Port-Olry, Vanuatu. USGS rated it Green, meaning low expected losses, and no damage was reported.

And the quiet day did not stay quiet. At 16:49 UTC on November 2, a magnitude 5.3 struck 81 km south-southeast of Palekastro, Crete, Greece, at a depth of about 48.5 km. No tsunami warning, watch, or advisory was issued.

Closer to home, a small magnitude 2.5 near Beersheba Springs, Tennessee, at 8:54 a.m. local time on November 2 — depth about 15.8 km — was felt by some people and caused no reported damage.

The preparedness part

We don't forecast earthquakes here — nobody can. We just read the instruments out loud. Quiet days are the best days to check the go-bag: water, food for 72 hours, a first-aid kit, a flashlight, and copies of your documents. The kit we keep coming back to in these watches is a compact waterproof first-aid and survival kit — the kind that lives in a car trunk or by the door and is forgotten until the day it isn't.

If today's log prompts you to check yours, that's the whole point of writing it down.
