Earthquake Watch — The Week's Biggest Quake Nobody Felt, and a Tiny One 522 People Did

By The Discovery Wire

USGS Community Internet Intensity Map

Five earthquakes of magnitude 6 or stronger struck the planet this week. The one the most people actually felt was a magnitude 2.7.

Here is the seismic log, straight from the public USGS catalog for the seven days ending November 3, 2026: 349 earthquakes of magnitude 2.5 or greater, 126 of them magnitude 4.5 or stronger, and five at magnitude 6 or above. Every one of the big ones carried a Green alert — low expected losses — because every one of them happened far from where people live, or far below them.

The week's largest

  • M6.4 southeast of Easter Island, Thu Oct 29 at 07:49 UTC, just 10 km deep — remote South Pacific ocean, no felt reports at all
  • M6.2 43 km east-southeast of Port-Olry, Vanuatu, Thu Oct 29 at 21:46 UTC, about 143 km deep, Green alert
  • M6.0 165 km northwest of Port-Olry, Vanuatu, Sun Nov 1 at 11:47 UTC, 10 km deep, Green alert
  • M6.0 58 km south-southeast of Shima, Japan, Sat Oct 31 at 22:15 UTC — but 352 km deep, which is why only 30 people reported feeling a magnitude 6
  • M6.0 214 km northwest of Port-Olry, Vanuatu, Wed Oct 28 at 02:21 UTC, Green alert

Vanuatu keeps appearing because that stretch of plate boundary has been restless for weeks — the same area took M6.9 and M6.3 events on October 24, and a tight five-quake cluster on October 28.

The quake people actually felt

The most-reported event of the week was nowhere near the largest. A magnitude 2.7, just 2 km northeast of Pacifica, California, at 00:17 UTC on October 29, about 6 km deep, drew 522 felt reports — more than every magnitude 6 on the list combined. It sat directly under the San Francisco Bay Area, where millions of people live close enough to notice a small, shallow nudge. The USGS intensity map above shows those 522 responses across 38 ZIP codes.

That is the week's real lesson, and it is the same one the instruments keep teaching: magnitude is a number about the quake. Intensity is a story about who was standing nearby.

The honest part

We don't forecast earthquakes here — nobody can. We just read the instruments out loud. A roundup like this is observation, not warning, and a quiet Green-alert week is not a promise about next week.

If today's log prompts you to check your go-bag — water, food for 72 hours, a first-aid kit, a flashlight — that is the whole point of writing it down.
