The Week's Biggest Quake Almost Nobody Felt

Every week the instruments have a story to tell, and this week's story is about the strange relationship between a quake's size and whether anyone notices it.

The week's largest: M5.9 off Indonesia

Saturday evening (6:55 PM EDT), the instruments logged the week's largest event: a magnitude 5.9, 39 km southwest of Tambolaka, Indonesia, at about 46 km depth. The USGS PAGER system rated it green — no expected casualties, no tsunami. Only a handful of "felt" reports came in, because the epicenter was far offshore. Big number, quiet outcome.

The most-felt: M4.2 in Washington state

The quake the most people actually felt was the modest one. On Tuesday, Sept 29 (5:05 PM EDT), a magnitude 4.2 struck 6 km west-northwest of Wauna, Washington, at about 28 km depth — right under the Puget Sound region. 9,549 people reported feeling it: the week's most-felt event by a wide margin, with light shaking (intensity IV) across Seattle, Tacoma, and Olympia.

The map above is the USGS Community Internet Intensity Map for that event — 9,549 responses plotted across 219 ZIP codes. The red star is the epicenter; the blue shading is where people felt the light shaking.

Elsewhere on the planet

  • M5.5, Volcano Islands (Japan region) — early this morning, remote and uninhabited; unfelt.
  • M5.3, 81 km SSW of Banda Aceh, Indonesia — Saturday evening; green alert.
  • M4.7, near Korumburra, Australia — Friday; the small number belies the strength: at just 10 km depth and 5 km from town, shaking reports reached intensity VI, felt by 517 people.

The tally

313 earthquakes of magnitude 2.5+ in seven days; 126 of them magnitude 4.5 or stronger. No M6+ anywhere on the planet this week. That's the Earth being the Earth — ringing like a bell, constantly, mostly where nobody is standing.

This is a roundup of public instrument readings — observation, not prediction. We don't forecast earthquakes, and nobody can. Source: public USGS-tracked earthquake feeds, week ending October 4, 2026.
